# Loss audit — the 358 body-inferred mehadrin claims

**Operation audited:** `kosherLevel: 'mehadrin'` → explicit `null` on 358 records.
`certifierId`, `kosherAuthorityGroup` and the raw `certifiedBy` text are **retained**.

**Why this file exists.** A cleanup that removes a structured claim has to record *where the affected
records came from*, *why they failed the evidence rules*, and *whether the information can be reacquired* —
not just a before/after count. Before-and-after counts tell you a number changed. They do not tell you what
to go and fix. This is the first audit produced under that standard, and reports like it are meant to
accumulate into an enrichment roadmap.

Machine-readable companion — per-record rows and every aggregate below:
`scripts/reports/audit-358-level-removal.json`, regenerated by
`node scripts/reports/audit-358-level-removal.mjs`.

Population definition, failure taxonomy and the registry evidence are in
[`KASHRUT_FACTS.md` §5b](KASHRUT_FACTS.md).

---

## 1. What is being removed, and why it fails the evidence rules

Every one of the 358 carries raw certification text that **names a certifying body and says nothing about a
level** — `בד״ץ בית יוסף`, `הרב לנדא`, `כשרות הרב מחפוד`. The registry recorded that fact by assigning those
aliases `level: null`. The mehadrin on these records was therefore never read from a source. It was produced
inside this project.

The registry's own behaviour is the standard being applied: across all 203 aliases, **80 carry
`level: 'mehadrin'` and all 80 contain a mehadrin word in their own raw text**, and **zero** aliases contain
such a word and were denied the level. The level field is a pure function of the text. Not once was a level
read off an authority.

## 2. Traceability of the loss — and the blocker

**The honest finding first: the dataset cannot currently answer "which source did this come from."**

| | |
|---|---|
| `source` field | **`'manual'` on all 358** |
| `provenance` entries | **0 of 358** |
| distinct `cityId` | 100 — geography **is** answerable |

`source: 'manual'` records that a human put the row there. It does not record where they got it. Dataset-wide
that value covers 1,978 records, and `source` has only six values in total, so this is not a gap specific to
the 358 — it is the shape of the field.

### A mechanism now exists — and it says so itself

`scripts/map-provenance.mjs` (added in `4db930c`) resolves an origin for every record into an additive
`sourceRef`, and it covers **358 / 358**. That is real and it should be used. But read its own
`confidence` field before treating it as an answer:

| `confidence` | n | |
|---|---|---|
| `derived` | **347** | resolved from the id shape — `manual-batch` 215, `numeric-legacy-batch` 100, `sourceUrl-host` 14, `leading-token` 11, `hashed-batch-id` 4, `id-prefix` 3 |
| `stated` | **11** | from `extra.dataSource` |

For 347 of the 358 the mapper performs **the same id inference this audit performed**. It standardises the
guess and makes it queryable — genuinely worth having — but it adds no evidence, and `sourceRef` must not be
read back as recorded provenance. The field is honestly designed: it labels its own confidence, and 97% of
these rows are labelled `derived`.

The 11 with a `stated` origin are **all humus-eli**, and their stated origin is `chain:humus-eli-yahoo` —
the business's own website. So of 358 records, exactly eleven record where they came from, and for those
eleven the answer is circular by the standard in §4.

**Attribution by id prefix is inference, not provenance,** and it is weak here. Grouping the 358 by the
leading segment of their ids gives **198 distinct prefixes, 173 of them singletons**:

| id prefix (inferred) | n |
|---|---|
| `manual-pizza` | 47 |
| `manual-br` | 27 |
| `manual-restaurant` | 16 |
| `humus-eli` | 11 |
| `burgerim` | 11 |
| `coffeetrail` | 10 |
| `manual-cafe` | 10 |
| `cafecafe` | 6 |
| `manual-golda` | 5 |
| `manual-winery` | 4 |
| …173 singletons | |

That long tail is itself the answer to "which source": **there wasn't one.** These records were curated one
at a time, not delivered by a small number of bulk importers. No amount of importer archaeology will produce
a source breakdown for 173 one-off rows, because there is no shared origin to find.

## 3. Failure classification — and a correction worth making

**All 358 classify as `BAD_INTERNAL_INFERENCE`.** None are `BAD_SOURCE`, `MISSING_SOURCE`,
`AMBIGUOUS_SOURCE` or `EXPIRED_SOURCE`.

That distinction carries the whole practical consequence: the upstream sources did not get the level wrong,
and they were not silent by accident. They named a body, which is exactly what a hechsher is. **Re-scraping
every one of these sources would change nothing**, because the thing that is wrong was never in them.

### But it is two inference sites, not one — and only one is fixed by fixing the migration

The natural reading is "our `MAP` in `migrate-kosher-fields.mjs` invented the level." That is true for 154
records. For the other 204 the level was already there before the migration ran, and the migration merely
propagated it.

| Site | n | `kosherType` | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — the migration MAP** | **154** | `badatz_beit_yosef` 126, `badatz_edah` 25, `rav_machpud` 3 | `kosherType` faithfully records the **body**. The MAP's `badatz_* → mehadrin` rows added a level the text never stated. |
| **B — upstream, before the MAP** | **204** | `mehadrin` 191, `rabanut_mehadrin` 13 | `kosherType` **already asserted a level**. The MAP only copied it across. |

The split correlates perfectly with `kosherAuthority`: 154/154 in site A carry one, **0/204** in site B do.

**Site B is the worse defect.** Its source text is `בד״ץ בית יוסף` (73×), `הרב לנדא` (20×), `רב מחפוד`
(16×) — text that names a body and no level. Whoever wrote `kosherType: 'mehadrin'` for those rows recorded
**a level the text did not state** while **discarding the body the text did state**, in one step. Both halves
wrong, in opposite directions.

The body is recoverable only because raw `certifiedBy` was preserved. This is the same identity-discarding
class as the humus-eli finding in FACTS, where 52 of 59 records were **not** recoverable — their
`certifiedBy` had already been overwritten with the bare word `מהדרין`.

> **Consequence for Batch B:** correcting the MAP prevents site A from recurring and does **nothing** about
> site B. 204 of the 358 were fabricated before the MAP was reached. Whatever guard is written has to sit at
> the point where `kosherType` is first assigned, not only where it is translated.

## 4. Recovery potential — a new source type is required

| | |
|---|---|
| `kosherCertUrl` | **0 / 358** |
| `certificateValidUntil` | **0 / 358** |
| `kosherDetails` | **0 / 358** |
| `sourceUrl` | 25 / 358 — **all 25 circular** |

The 25 resolve to four domains: `humus-eli-yahoo.com` (11), `coffeetrail.co.il` (10), `cafecafe.co.il` (3),
`gushetzion-winery.co.il` (1). Every one is the page the record was scraped **from**. Citing the source you
copied is not corroboration — the same circularity as `import-golda.mjs`, which scraped goldaglida.co.il and
would "verify" Golda against it.

**So recovery is not a matter of using existing sources better. It requires a source type this project does
not have for these bodies: the certificate document itself.** A hechsher is issued per location and states
its own standard; nothing short of it establishes a level for a specific branch.

**The pattern already exists and works.** Tzohar: 180 certificate URLs, 164 expiry dates, extracted from real
PDFs. What blocks replicating it is recorded in `src/types/place.ts` — Tzohar is *"the one certifying body we
have permission to publish certificate data for."* **The blocker is permission, not engineering.**

Five bodies cover 347 of the 358, so the roadmap is short and concrete:

| Authority | records | What would establish a level |
|---|---|---|
| `badatz-beit-yosef` | 225 | per-branch certificate; publication permission |
| `badatz-edah-hachareidis` | 39 | ″ |
| `rav-machpud` | 36 | ″ |
| `rav-landau` | 35 | ″ |
| `rav-rubin` | 12 | ″ |
| 9 others (7 rabbinates + 2 badatz) | 11 | ″ |

Worth noting for whoever pursues this: the one certificate pipeline that does exist produced **`regular` on
all 180 of its records**. Having a certificate has so far been perfectly anti-correlated with claiming
mehadrin. Nobody should assume these 358 come back as mehadrin once documents arrive.

## 5. Which regions get materially weaker

100 cities are affected. The distribution is not flat, and the concentration is the point.

| City | lost | had | % of its mehadrin coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **בני ברק** | 29 | 38 | **76%** |
| **בית שמש** | 18 | 31 | **58%** |
| ירושלים | 18 | 114 | 16% |
| פתח תקווה | 16 | 41 | 39% |
| תל אביב | 14 | 41 | 34% |
| ראשון לציון | 13 | 31 | 42% |
| נתיבות | 11 | 24 | 46% |
| באר שבע | 11 | 22 | 50% |
| חולון | 9 | 20 | 45% |
| אשקלון | 8 | 16 | 50% |

**בני ברק is the headline.** It loses 76% of its mehadrin coverage, and it is the city where the mehadrin
filter carries the most weight for the most users. That is an enrichment target rather than a regression —
but it is the one that should be funded first, and the one most likely to be noticed.

**25 cities lose 100% of their mehadrin coverage** (28 records): קריית מוצקין (3), אזור (2), and 23 with a
single record each — כרמיאל, ביתר עלית, כפר חב״ד, מזכרת בתיה among them. These are places where the app
currently answers "yes, there is mehadrin here" on the strength of one invented claim.

## 6. The missing mechanism

`provenance` already exists on 3,092 records with exactly the right shape — per-field attribution naming the
supplying pipeline:

```json
{"enrichedBy":"religious-council","council":"המועצה הדתית נתניה","sourceId":"rc-netanya-12701","fields":["phone","address"]}
{"enrichedBy":"arcgis-municipal","source":"arcgis:herzliya","sourceRecordId":"arcgis:herzliya:66","fields":["address"]}
```

Across all 3,092 entries, `fields` **never once** contains a kashrut field. The mechanism this audit needed
was already built, and was applied to phone numbers and addresses but not to the claims that carry religious
weight.

Recommended scope: per-field provenance for the **derived** kashrut fields specifically — `certifierId`,
`kosherLevel`, `kosherAuthorityGroup` — because those are the fields that get *computed*, and therefore the
fields where "what was this derived from" is a question that will be asked again. Extending it to all 7,471
records and every field is a larger change than the problem requires.
